11  Issuing, serving, pleading and responding

Assessment objective 4: commencing, responding to or progressing a claim.

11.1 Issuing the claim form

Proceedings are started when the court issues a claim form at the claimant’s request (CPR 7.2) — but for limitation purposes the claim is brought when the claim form is received by the court office. Both propositions matter, and they answer different questions.

The Part 7 procedure is the standard route for a disputed claim. Part 8 is for claims where there is unlikely to be a substantial dispute of fact, and where the rules require or permit it.

11.1.1 The claim form (N1)

CPR 16.2 requires the claim form to:

  • name the parties correctly;
  • contain a concise statement of the nature of the claim;
  • specify the remedy sought — though the court may grant a remedy the claimant has not asked for;
  • where money is claimed, contain a statement of value (CPR 16.3): the amount claimed, or a statement that the claimant expects to recover not more than £10,000, more than £10,000 but not more than £25,000, more than £25,000 but not more than £100,000, or more than £100,000 — and, if issued in the High Court, why the claimant is entitled to issue there;
  • be verified by a statement of truth.

The court fee is calculated on the value claimed, so pleading the claim too high is expensive and pleading it too low caps recovery.

11.1.2 Naming the parties

Defendant Named as
Individual Full name, with title
Sole trader “Name trading as Business Name”
Partnership The firm name, where the partners carried on business under it when the cause of action accrued
Company The full registered name, including “Limited” or “plc”
LLP The full registered name
Child or protected party By or through a litigation friend
Estate The personal representatives, or if none, an order appointing a person to represent the estate

11.1.3 Adding, removing and substituting parties

CPR 19.2: the court may remove, add or substitute a party where it is desirable so that the court can resolve all matters in dispute, or where there is an issue between an existing party and the new party which it is desirable to resolve.

CPR 19.4: the court’s permission is required to add or substitute a party after the claim form has been served. Before service, the claimant may amend as of right.

CPR 19.6 — after limitation has expired. This is the restrictive rule, and it is examined. The court may add or substitute a party only if:

  • the relevant limitation period was current when the proceedings were started; and
  • the addition or substitution is necessary.

It is “necessary” only if the court is satisfied that:

  1. the new party is to be substituted for a party who was named in the claim form by mistake for the new party;
  2. the claim cannot properly be carried on by or against the original party unless the new party is added or substituted; or
  3. the original party has died or had a bankruptcy order made against them and their interest or liability has passed to the new party.
Exam trap

The mistake limb in CPR 19.6(3)(a) covers a mistake as to name, not a change of mind about who to sue. Naming “ABC Trading Ltd” when the contracting party was “ABC Trading (Northern) Ltd” is a mistake. Deciding, after limitation has expired, that you should have sued the director personally as well as the company is not — that is a new claim against a new party, and CPR 19.6 will not permit it.

11.2 Serving the claim form

11.2.1 The time limit

CPR 7.5: the claimant must complete the step required by 12.00 midnight on the calendar day four months after the date of issue of the claim form. Where the claim form is to be served out of the jurisdiction, the period is six months (CPR 7.5(2)).

The “step required” depends on the method:

Method Step required
First class post, DX or other next business day delivery Posting, leaving with, delivering to or collection by the relevant service provider
Delivery to or leaving at a permitted address Delivering to or leaving the document at the relevant place
Personal service under CPR 6.5 Leaving it with the individual
Electronic method Sending the email or other transmission
Exam trap

Under CPR 7.5 the claimant must complete the step, not achieve service, within four months. Posting the claim form by first class post on the last day is compliant even though deemed service falls afterwards. This is the single most frequently misunderstood rule in the service topic.

11.2.2 Extending time for service

CPR 7.6. An application to extend must generally be made within the period for service. If made after it has expired, the court may only extend if:

  • the court has failed to serve the claim form; or
  • the claimant has taken all reasonable steps to comply with CPR 7.5 but has been unable to do so; and
  • in either case, the claimant has acted promptly in making the application.

This is deliberately severe, because an extension after limitation has expired deprives the defendant of an accrued limitation defence.

11.2.3 The methods and the address

CPR 6.3 — permitted methods: personal service; first class post, DX or other next business day delivery service; leaving it at a specified place; fax or other means of electronic communication; or any method authorised by the court.

Exam trap

Service by email is valid only where the party to be served, or their solicitor, has indicated in writing that they are willing to accept service by that means and given an address for it. In Barton v Wright Hassall LLP [2018] UKSC 12 a litigant in person emailed the claim form to the defendant’s solicitors on the last day, without having established their willingness to accept email service. The Supreme Court refused to validate it retrospectively under CPR 6.15(2), and the claim was lost.

Who to serve (CPR 6.7): if the defendant has given in writing the business address of a solicitor authorised to accept service, the claim form must be served on that solicitor. Otherwise CPR 6.9 supplies the place:

Defendant Place of service
Individual Usual or last known residence
Individual being sued in the name of a business Usual or last known residence, or the principal or last known place of business
Partnership sued in the firm name Usual or last known residence of a partner, or the principal or last known place of business of the partnership
Company registered in England and Wales The principal office, or any place of business having a real connection with the claim

A company may alternatively be served at its registered office under s.1139 Companies Act 2006.

11.2.4 Deemed service

CPR 6.14 — the claim form. A claim form served within the United Kingdom is deemed to be served on the second business day after completion of the relevant step under CPR 7.5(1). There is a single rule and no method-by-method table, whatever the actual date of delivery.

CPR 6.26 — every other document. Here there is a table:

Method Deemed date of service
First class post, DX or other next business day delivery The second day after posting, if that is a business day; otherwise the next business day after that day
Delivering to or leaving at a permitted address If before 4.30 p.m. on a business day, that day; otherwise the next business day
Personal service Same rule as above
Fax Same rule as above
Other electronic method Same rule as above
Worked example

A claim form is issued on Monday 4 May. The claimant posts it by first class post on Friday 28 August.

Is service in time? The four-month period expires at midnight on 4 September. The step required for postal service is posting, which happened on 28 August. Service is in time.

When is it deemed served? CPR 6.14: the second business day after completion of the step. Posting was Friday; the first business day after is Monday 31 August, the second is Tuesday 1 September. Deemed service is Tuesday 1 September, whatever day it actually arrived.

When is the defence due? Fourteen days from service of the particulars of claim. If the particulars were served with the claim form, an acknowledgment of service or defence is due by 15 September; filing an acknowledgment extends the defence to 29 September.

11.2.5 Service by an alternative method

CPR 6.15. Where there is a good reason, the court may authorise service by a method or at a place not otherwise permitted. Under CPR 6.15(2) it may also, on the same test, order that steps already taken to bring the claim form to the defendant’s attention are to be treated as good service. The order must specify the method, the deemed date of service, and the period for responding.

11.2.6 Service out of the jurisdiction

Two routes, and the distinction decides the question:

Without permission (CPR 6.32–6.33). Available where the English court has power to determine the claim under the Civil Jurisdiction and Judgments Act 1982, where the contract contains a term to the effect that the English court shall have jurisdiction, or under the Hague 2005 Convention.

With permission (CPR 6.36). The claimant must satisfy the three requirements set out in Chapter 10: a good arguable case within a PD 6B gateway, a serious issue to be tried, and that England is the proper place. The application is made without notice, supported by evidence, and the claimant owes a duty of full and frank disclosure.

Mechanics abroad. Service may be effected through the foreign government or judicial authority or the British consular authority (CPR 6.42), through a Hague Service Convention channel where the destination state is a party, or by any method permitted by the law of the country in which service is to be effected (CPR 6.40(3)) — but never by a method that is contrary to that country’s law. The period for responding is extended by PD 6B according to the destination.

11.3 Statements of case

11.3.1 Particulars of claim

CPR 16.4: a concise statement of the facts on which the claimant relies; a claim for interest if any, with the basis and, for a specified sum, the percentage rate, the dates from and to which it is claimed and the daily rate; any claim for aggravated or exemplary damages or provisional damages; and a statement of truth.

The particulars must be served with the claim form or within 14 days after service of the claim form (CPR 7.4), and in any event within the four-month period for serving the claim form.

The content follows the cause of action:

Contract claim Tort (negligence) claim
The status of the parties The status of the parties
The contract: date, parties, whether written or oral, consideration The facts giving rise to a duty of care
The terms relied on, express or implied The duty owed
Performance by the claimant The acts or omissions constituting breach
The breach, with particulars Causation
Causation and loss, with a schedule of loss The damage, with a schedule of loss
Interest Interest

11.3.2 Defence

CPR 16.5 requires the defendant to state, in relation to each allegation, whether it is:

  • denied — and if so, the defendant must state the reasons for the denial and, if putting forward a different version of events, state that version;
  • admitted; or
  • neither admitted nor denied, because the defendant is unable to admit or deny it and requires the claimant to prove it.

Failure to deal with an allegation is a deemed admission — except that a defendant who fails to deal with an allegation but has set out the nature of their case in relation to the issue is taken to require it to be proved, and a defendant who fails to deal with an allegation about the amount of money claimed is taken to require it to be proved. The defence must dispute the claimant’s statement of value if the defendant disputes it, and must give its own.

The defence must also plead any positive case and any limitation defence, and must be verified by a statement of truth.

11.3.3 Reply, Part 20 claims and further information

Reply (CPR 16.7). Optional. A claimant who does not file one is not taken to admit the matters raised in the defence. A reply is needed to answer a positive case in the defence that the particulars of claim do not already address.

Additional claims under Part 20:

Claim Permission needed?
Counterclaim against the claimant No, if filed with the defence; otherwise yes (CPR 20.4)
Counterclaim against a person other than the claimant Yes (CPR 20.5)
Claim for contribution or indemnity against a co-defendant No, if filed with the defence, or within 28 days of a party being added (CPR 20.6)
Any other additional claim — for example against a third party No, if issued before or at the same time as the defence is filed; otherwise yes (CPR 20.7)

An additional claim is treated as a claim in its own right, and Part 20 applies the ordinary rules to it with modifications.

Further information (CPR 18). A party may serve a written request for clarification of any matter in dispute or additional information about it, giving a date for response. If the other party does not comply, an application may be made. The request must be concise and strictly confined to what is reasonably necessary and proportionate.

11.3.4 Amendments

CPR 17.1: a party may amend a statement of case at any time before it has been served on any other party. After service, amendment requires the written consent of all the other parties or the permission of the court.

CPR 17.4 — amendment after limitation has expired. Where the relevant limitation period was current when the proceedings started, the court may allow an amendment only:

  • to add or substitute a new claim arising out of the same facts or substantially the same facts as a claim already made; or
  • to correct a mistake as to the name of a party, where the mistake was genuine and not one which would cause reasonable doubt as to the identity of the party; or
  • to alter the capacity in which a party claims.

The court will also consider whether the amendment is in accordance with the overriding objective — lateness, the effect on the trial date and the costs consequences all bear on the discretion.

11.3.5 The statement of truth

Every statement of case must be verified by a statement of truth. Since April 2020 the prescribed wording is:

I believe that the facts stated in this [document] are true. I understand that proceedings for contempt of court may be brought against anyone who makes, or causes to be made, a false statement in a document verified by a statement of truth without an honest belief in its truth.

It may be signed by the party, by a person with authority (for a company, a person holding a senior position, who must state which), or by the party’s legal representative — who signs on the party’s behalf and must have the party’s authority and have explained the consequences.

Exam trap

A solicitor may sign a statement of truth on the client’s behalf, but may never verify a statement the solicitor knows or believes to be false. Where the client insists on advancing an account the solicitor knows to be untrue, the solicitor must not draft or sign it, and will usually have to cease to act — without disclosing the reason, because the reason is confidential.

11.4 Responding to a claim

11.4.1 The time limits

Step Time limit
Acknowledgment of service 14 days after service of the particulars of claim (CPR 10.3)
Defence, where no acknowledgment filed 14 days after service of the particulars of claim (CPR 15.4)
Defence, where an acknowledgment has been filed 28 days after service of the particulars of claim
Agreed extension of the defence Up to a further 28 days, by agreement, notifying the court in writing (CPR 15.5) — so a maximum of 56 days without a court order
Application disputing jurisdiction 14 days after filing the acknowledgment of service (CPR 11)

11.4.2 The options

Admit (Part 14). The defendant may admit the whole or part of the claim. For a specified sum, the claimant may then obtain judgment; the defendant may ask for time to pay, and the rate of payment is determined by the court or by agreement.

Defend. File an acknowledgment of service and/or a defence, and consider a counterclaim.

Dispute the court’s jurisdiction (Part 11). The defendant must first file an acknowledgment of service — filing one is not a submission to the jurisdiction — and then apply within 14 days. If the defendant files a defence without applying, they are treated as having accepted that the court has jurisdiction.

Do nothing — and face default judgment.

Exam trap

To challenge jurisdiction, the defendant must acknowledge service and then apply within 14 days. Doing nothing loses the point (default judgment follows) and filing a defence loses it too (submission). The acknowledgment of service form has a tick box for “I intend to contest jurisdiction” precisely for this purpose.

11.4.3 Default judgment

Part 12. The claimant may obtain judgment in default where the defendant has failed to file an acknowledgment of service or a defence, and the time for doing so has expired. It is entered administratively on request for a specified sum, and on application otherwise.

Default judgment is not available where:

  • the claim is under Part 8;
  • the defendant has applied for summary judgment or to have the claim struck out, and the application has not been disposed of;
  • the defendant has satisfied the claim; or
  • the defendant has admitted the claim and applied for time to pay.

Setting aside (Part 13):

  • CPR 13.2 — mandatory. The court must set the judgment aside if it was wrongly entered — because the time for responding had not in fact expired, or the claim had been satisfied, or the defendant had filed an acknowledgment or defence in time.
  • CPR 13.3 — discretionary. The court may set it aside if the defendant has a real prospect of successfully defending the claim, or if it appears to the court that there is some other good reason why the judgment should be set aside or the defendant should be allowed to defend. On any such application the court must have regard to whether the application was made promptly.
Worked example

Particulars of claim are deemed served on 1 October. The defendant, who has been abroad, files nothing. On 20 October the claimant enters default judgment. The defendant returns on 5 November, and applies to set the judgment aside on 20 December, saying the goods were defective.

Was the judgment properly entered? The defence was due by 15 October and nothing was filed, so yes. CPR 13.2 does not apply.

Will it be set aside? Only under CPR 13.3, and the defendant must show a real prospect of successfully defending — a defective goods defence may well qualify. But the court must have regard to promptness, and a six-week delay after returning is a serious obstacle. On these facts the application may fail on delay alone, and if it succeeds the defendant will almost certainly bear the costs thrown away.

11.4.4 Discontinuance and settlement

Discontinuance (Part 38). The claimant may discontinue at any time by filing and serving a notice of discontinuance. Permission is required where the court has granted an interim injunction or where any party has given an undertaking to the court, or where the claimant has received an interim payment. Where there is more than one claimant, the consent of the others or the court’s permission is needed. Unless the court orders otherwise, the claimant is liable for the defendant’s costs to the date of discontinuance (CPR 38.6).

Settlement may be recorded by:

  • a consent order, where the terms are to be enforceable as an order of the court and there is nothing confidential;
  • a Tomlin order — an order staying the proceedings on agreed terms set out in a schedule, with permission to apply for the purpose of carrying those terms into effect. The schedule is not part of the order and need not be a matter of public record, which is why it is used where the terms are confidential or go beyond what the court could order;
  • acceptance of a Part 36 offer, which stays the claim automatically; or
  • a simple settlement agreement, with the claim then discontinued.

11.5 Check yourself

Q1. A claim form is issued on 3 March. On 30 June the claimant’s solicitor posts it to the defendant’s solicitors, who are authorised in writing to accept service, by first class post. It arrives on 2 July.

Which of the following is correct?

A. Service is out of time, because the claim form was not received by the defendant’s solicitors within four months of issue.

B. Service is in time: the step required for postal service is posting, which was completed on 30 June, within four months of issue; the claim form is deemed served on the second business day after posting.

C. Service is in time, and the claim form is deemed served on the day it arrived, 2 July.

D. Service is out of time, because service on solicitors is not permitted.

E. Service is in time, and the claim form is deemed served on the day it was posted, 30 June.

Q2. Particulars of claim are deemed served on a defendant on Tuesday 6 October. The defendant files an acknowledgment of service on 15 October, indicating an intention to defend.

By when must the defence be filed, assuming no agreement or order extending time?

A. 20 October, being 14 days after the acknowledgment of service.

B. 20 October, being 14 days after service of the particulars of claim.

C. 3 November, being 28 days after service of the particulars of claim.

D. 12 November, being 28 days after the acknowledgment of service.

E. 1 December, being 56 days after service of the particulars of claim.

Q3. A claimant sued “Northgate Logistics Limited”. The contracting party was in fact “Northgate Logistics (UK) Limited”, a different company in the same group with a similar name. The limitation period expired two months after the claim form was issued. The claimant now applies to substitute the correct company.

Which of the following is correct?

A. The application must be refused, because the limitation period has expired.

B. The application may be granted under CPR 19.6 if the limitation period was current when the proceedings were started and the substitution is necessary — which it is where the new party is being substituted for a party named in the claim form by mistake for the new party.

C. The application may be granted as of right, because the claim form has not yet been amended.

D. The application must be refused, because the claimant chose to sue a different legal person and that is not a mistake as to name.

E. The claimant must issue fresh proceedings and rely on s.33 of the Limitation Act 1980.

Q4. A defendant based in Germany is served with an English claim form. The defendant believes the English court has no jurisdiction because the contract contains an exclusive German jurisdiction clause.

What must the defendant do to preserve the point?

A. File a defence setting out the jurisdiction objection.

B. Write to the court explaining that it does not accept jurisdiction, and take no further step.

C. File an acknowledgment of service indicating an intention to contest jurisdiction, and apply under Part 11 within 14 days after filing it.

D. Apply for summary judgment under Part 24.

E. Ignore the proceedings, because a foreign defendant cannot be bound by an English judgment.

Q5. A defendant, having filed a defence, wishes to bring a claim for an indemnity against a sub-contractor who is not a party to the proceedings. The defence was filed three weeks ago.

Which of the following is correct?

A. The defendant may issue the additional claim without permission at any time.

B. The defendant requires the court’s permission, because the additional claim was not issued before or at the same time as the defence was filed.

C. The defendant may only bring the claim in separate proceedings.

D. The defendant requires the consent of the claimant.

E. The defendant may issue the additional claim without permission because it is a claim for an indemnity.

Answers and explanations

Q1 — B. CPR 7.5(1): the claimant must complete the step required within four months of issue, and for first class post that step is posting. Posting on 30 June, from a claim form issued on 3 March, is within four months. Deemed service under CPR 6.14 is the second business day after completion of the step, regardless of actual delivery.

  • A and D apply the wrong test: what matters is the step, not receipt, and CPR 6.7 requires service on a solicitor authorised in writing to accept it.
  • C applies the actual date of delivery. CPR 6.14 is a deeming provision and the real date is irrelevant.
  • E deems service on the day of the step, which is what CPR 6.14 expressly does not do.

Q2 — C. CPR 15.4(1)(b): where the defendant files an acknowledgment of service, the defence must be filed 28 days after service of the particulars of claim — not 28 days after the acknowledgment. Twenty-eight days from 6 October is 3 November.

  • A and D run time from the acknowledgment, which is the classic error.
  • B applies the 14-day period that operates only where no acknowledgment is filed.
  • E applies the maximum available with an agreed extension under CPR 15.5, which the facts exclude.

Q3 — B. CPR 19.6. The limitation period was current when the proceedings were started, and substitution is “necessary” under CPR 19.6(3)(a) where the new party is to be substituted for a party named in the claim form by mistake for the new party. Suing the wrong company in a group because of a similar name is a classic mistake as to identity of this kind, provided the mistake was genuine and would not cause reasonable doubt as to who was intended.

  • A treats the expiry of limitation as an absolute bar, which CPR 19.6 exists to qualify.
  • C ignores the need for permission after service and the special rule after limitation.
  • D is the strongest distractor, and would be right if the claimant had deliberately sued one legal person and later changed its mind. Here the intention was always to sue the contracting party.
  • E applies s.33, which is available only in personal injury and fatal accident claims.

Q4 — C. CPR Part 11. The defendant must file an acknowledgment of service — which is not a submission to the jurisdiction — and then apply for an order declaring that the court has no jurisdiction, or should not exercise it, within 14 days after filing the acknowledgment.

  • A is fatal: filing a defence without making the application means the defendant is treated as having accepted that the court has jurisdiction.
  • B and E invite default judgment.
  • D attacks the merits, not the forum, and engaging with the merits risks submission.

Q5 — B. CPR 20.7(3): an additional claim other than a counterclaim against the claimant or a claim for contribution or indemnity against an existing co-defendant may be made without permission only if it is issued before or at the same time as the defendant files their defence. The defence was filed three weeks ago, so permission is required.

  • A and E overlook the timing condition. Note the distinction being tested: CPR 20.6 allows a contribution or indemnity claim against an existing co-defendant without permission if filed with the defence; this sub-contractor is not yet a party, so CPR 20.7 applies.
  • C is wrong: Part 20 exists so that related claims can be tried together.
  • D requires a consent the rules do not call for.
