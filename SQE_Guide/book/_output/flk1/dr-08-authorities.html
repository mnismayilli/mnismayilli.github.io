17  Dispute Resolution: authorities, rules and numbers

How to use this chapter

Dispute Resolution is a procedure subject, so unlike Business Law and Practice the numbers here really do have to be learned. Rule numbers matter less than the rule itself — but the time limits, thresholds and tests are the substance of the questions.

The SRA does not require case citations except where the case name is the term practitioners use for the principle. American Cyanamid, Denton, Spiliada, Churchill and Ladd v Marshall all qualify.

17.1 Cases

Case Proposition
Churchill v Merthyr Tydfil County Borough Council [2023] EWCA Civ 1416 The court can stay proceedings and order the parties to engage in non-court dispute resolution, provided it does not impair the right to a judicial hearing and is proportionate. The Halsey passage to the contrary was not binding
Halsey v Milton Keynes General NHS Trust [2004] EWCA Civ 576 A successful party who unreasonably refuses ADR may be deprived of costs; sets out the factors for assessing reasonableness
Spiliada Maritime Corp v Cansulex Ltd [1987] AC 460 Forum non conveniens: the court stays proceedings where another available forum is clearly or distinctly more appropriate — the “natural forum”
FS Cairo (Nile Plaza) LLC v Brownlie [2021] UKSC 45 “Damage” in the tort jurisdiction gateway means any significant damage sustained in England, not only the initial damage
Barton v Wright Hassall LLP [2018] UKSC 12 Service by email without the recipient’s written indication of willingness is invalid, and will not readily be validated retrospectively under CPR 6.15(2)
Denton v TH White Ltd [2014] EWCA Civ 906 Relief from sanctions: (1) seriousness and significance of the breach; (2) why it occurred; (3) all the circumstances, giving particular weight to CPR 3.9(1)(a) and (b)
Mitchell v News Group Newspapers Ltd [2013] EWCA Civ 1537 The over-strict predecessor to Denton, corrected by it
Swain v Hillman [2001] 1 All ER 91 “Real prospect of success” means realistic, not fanciful; no mini-trial on a summary judgment application
Easyair Ltd v Opal Telecom Ltd [2009] EWHC 339 (Ch) Collects the summary judgment principles, including the treatment of evidence expected to be available at trial
American Cyanamid Co v Ethicon Ltd [1975] AC 396 Interim injunctions: serious question to be tried; adequacy of damages; balance of convenience; status quo
Waugh v British Railways Board [1980] AC 521 Litigation privilege requires litigation to be the dominant purpose
Three Rivers DC v Bank of England (No 5) [2003] EWCA Civ 474 For legal advice privilege, the “client” is confined to those tasked with seeking and receiving the advice
Three Rivers DC v Bank of England (No 6) [2004] UKHL 48 “Legal advice” extends to advice on what should prudently and sensibly be done in the relevant legal context
SFO v Eurasian Natural Resources Corporation Ltd [2018] EWCA Civ 2006 A criminal investigation may put litigation in reasonable contemplation; documents created to obtain advice on avoiding or settling litigation are covered
Rush & Tompkins Ltd v GLC [1989] AC 1280 Without prejudice protection extends to other parties to the same litigation and survives settlement
Unilever plc v Procter & Gamble Co [2000] 1 WLR 2436 The exceptions to without prejudice privilege, including unambiguous impropriety
Ladd v Marshall [1954] 1 WLR 1489 New evidence on appeal: unobtainable with reasonable diligence; probably an important influence; apparently credible
Dymocks Franchise Systems (NSW) Pty Ltd v Todd [2004] UKPC 39 Non-party costs: the touchstone is whether the non-party is the “real party” — funding and controlling or benefiting
Ridehalgh v Horsefield [1994] Ch 205 Wasted costs: improper, unreasonable or negligent conduct; causation; and whether it is just to make the order
Ho v Adelekun [2021] UKSC 43 and Cartwright v Venduct Engineering Ltd [2018] EWCA Civ 1654 Both reversed by the April 2023 amendment to CPR 44.14
R (PACCAR Inc) v Competition Appeal Tribunal [2023] UKSC 28 A litigation funding agreement giving the funder a share of damages is a DBA, and unenforceable unless compliant
Henderson v Henderson (1843) 3 Hare 100 Relitigating a point that could and should have been raised is an abuse of process

17.3 The numbers

17.3.1 Time limits

Period What it is
3 clear days Notice of an interim application (CPR 23.7)
7 days Service of a witness summons before attendance; respondent’s evidence before a summary judgment hearing; trial bundle earliest filing
3 days Trial bundle latest filing; evidence in reply on a summary judgment application
24 hours Statement of costs before a hearing to be summarily assessed
14 days Acknowledgment of service; defence where no acknowledgment; application disputing jurisdiction after acknowledgment; notice of a summary judgment hearing; compliance with a money judgment; service of a CPR 71 order before the hearing
21 days Relevant period on a Part 36 offer (minimum); appellant’s notice; points of dispute; costs budgets before the first CMC
28 days Defence where an acknowledgment has been filed; written questions to an expert after service of the report; agreed extension of a defence (maximum further period); challenge to a CVA or IVA
56 days Maximum for a defence by agreement, without a court order
4 months Service of the claim form within the jurisdiction
6 months Service of the claim form outside the jurisdiction
3 months Commencing detailed assessment
30 weeks Typical fast track trial from allocation
7 clear days Notice of enforcement before taking control of goods

17.3.2 Money thresholds

Figure What it is
£10,000 Small claims track limit; compulsory Small Claims Mediation Service
£1,000 / £5,000 Small claims track: general damages limit for personal injury / RTA whiplash
£25,000 Fast track upper limit
£100,000 Intermediate track upper limit; High Court money claim threshold
£50,000 High Court personal injury threshold; costs budget filed with the DQ below this value
£10 million Costs budgeting does not apply at or above this value
£500,000 / £60,000 IPEC damages cap / costs cap on liability (£30,000 on quantum)
£75,000 Cap on the Part 36 additional amount
10% / 5% The Part 36 additional amount: 10% of the first £500,000, 5% thereafter
10% above base rate Maximum enhanced interest under CPR 36.17(4); maximum interest penalty for protocol breach
8% Post-judgment interest
£600 / £5,000 Enforcement against goods: under £600 County Court only; £5,000 or more High Court only; in between, either (CCA-regulated judgments always County Court)
£5,000 County Court judgments at or above this carry interest

17.3.3 The tests

Test Where
No real prospect of success and no other compelling reason Summary judgment, CPR 24.3
Real prospect of success or some other compelling reason Permission to appeal, CPR 52.6
Real prospect of success AND an important point of principle or practice, or some other compelling reason Second appeals, CPR 52.7
Wrong, or unjust because of a serious procedural or other irregularity Grounds of appeal, CPR 52.21(3)
Real prospect of successfully defending, or some other good reason — with regard to promptness Setting aside default judgment, CPR 13.3
Serious question to be tried; damages inadequate; balance of convenience; status quo Interim injunction, American Cyanamid
Good arguable case; real risk of dissipation Freezing injunction
Seriousness and significance; why; all the circumstances Relief from sanctions, Denton
Good arguable case in a gateway; serious issue to be tried; England the proper place Permission to serve out
Clearly or distinctly more appropriate forum Forum non conveniens, Spiliada
Dominant purpose Litigation privilege, Waugh
A reasonable proportion of the likely amount of the final judgment Interim payments, CPR 25.20
