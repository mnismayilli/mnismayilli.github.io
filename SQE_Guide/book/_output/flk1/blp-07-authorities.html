8  Authorities and numbers

How to use this chapter

The SRA does not require you to recall case citations, except where the case name is itself the term practitioners use for the principle. This chapter exists so that you can revise the propositions quickly and see how they fit together, not so that you can memorise citations. The right-hand column is what earns the mark.

The numbers, by contrast, are worth knowing — with one exception. Tax rates and thresholds will normally be given to you in the question. Everything else in the tables below — notice periods, filing deadlines, voting thresholds, look-back periods — will not be.

8.1 Cases

8.1.1 Legal personality and the veil

Case Proposition
Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd [1897] AC 22 A properly incorporated company is a legal person distinct from its members, even where one person owns and controls it
Macaura v Northern Assurance Co Ltd [1925] AC 619 A shareholder has no insurable interest in the company’s assets
Lee v Lee’s Air Farming Ltd [1961] AC 12 A person may be controlling shareholder, sole director and employee of the same company
Adams v Cape Industries plc [1990] Ch 433 There is no single economic unit doctrine; a parent is not liable for a subsidiary’s debts merely because of the group relationship
Prest v Petrodel Resources Ltd [2013] UKSC 34 The veil is pierced only on the evasion principle — a pre-existing obligation deliberately frustrated by interposing a company. Concealment is ordinary fact-finding, not piercing
Gilford Motor Co Ltd v Horne [1933] Ch 935 Company formed to evade a restrictive covenant: relief against both
Jones v Lipman [1962] 1 WLR 832 Company used to defeat specific performance: order against both

8.1.2 Formation and the constitution

Case Proposition
Kelner v Baxter (1866) LR 2 CP 174 A company cannot ratify a pre-incorporation contract
Phonogram Ltd v Lane [1982] QB 938 Personal liability under what is now s.51 arises even where both parties knew the company did not exist; “for and on behalf of” is not a clear exclusion
Braymist Ltd v Wise Finance Co Ltd [2002] EWCA Civ 127 The person who purported to act may also enforce the contract
Hickman v Kent or Romney Marsh Sheep-Breeders’ Association [1915] 1 Ch 881 The s.33 contract binds member to company
Pender v Lushington (1877) 6 Ch D 70 It binds company to member as to membership rights (here, the right to vote)
Rayfield v Hands [1960] Ch 1 It may bind member to member directly
Eley v Positive Government Security Life Assurance Co (1876) 1 Ex D 88 It is enforceable only in respect of rights held qua member
Allen v Gold Reefs of West Africa Ltd [1900] 1 Ch 656 The power to alter the articles must be exercised bona fide for the benefit of the company as a whole
Citco Banking Corporation NV v Pusser’s Ltd [2007] UKPC 13 The test is whether no reasonable person could consider the alteration to be for the company’s benefit; incidental benefit to the majority does not invalidate it
Russell v Northern Bank Development Corporation Ltd [1992] 1 WLR 588 The company cannot contract out of its statutory powers; shareholders may bind themselves as to how they will vote
Re Duomatic Ltd [1969] 2 Ch 365 The unanimous informal assent of all members entitled to vote is as effective as a formal resolution

8.1.3 Directors and their duties

Case Proposition
Bushell v Faith [1970] AC 1099 Weighted voting rights on a removal resolution are valid; s.168 requires only an ordinary resolution
Howard Smith Ltd v Ampol Petroleum Ltd [1974] AC 821 The power to allot shares must be used for its proper purpose — raising capital, not manipulating control
Eclairs Group Ltd v JKX Oil & Gas plc [2015] UKSC 71 Where purposes are mixed, a “but for” causative improper purpose invalidates the exercise
Re Smith & Fawcett Ltd [1942] Ch 304 Directors must act bona fide in what they consider is in the company’s interests: the s.172 test is subjective
Charterbridge Corporation Ltd v Lloyds Bank Ltd [1970] Ch 62 Where the director gave the matter no thought, the court asks objectively whether an honest and intelligent person could reasonably have believed it was for the company’s benefit
BTI 2014 LLC v Sequana SA [2022] UKSC 25 The creditor duty modifies s.172; engaged when the company is insolvent or bordering on insolvency, or an insolvent liquidation or administration is probable; owed to the company
Re D’Jan of London Ltd [1994] 1 BCLC 561 The s.174 standard is dual objective/subjective; s.1157 relief may be granted where the director acted honestly and reasonably
Re Barings plc (No 5) [1999] 1 BCLC 433 Delegation is permissible; the residual duty to supervise is not delegable
Aberdeen Railway Co v Blaikie Brothers (1854) 1 Macq 461 No fiduciary may place himself in a position where duty and interest conflict
Regal (Hastings) Ltd v Gulliver [1967] 2 AC 134 Liability to account arises from the fact of the profit; good faith, company benefit and the company’s inability to take the opportunity are all irrelevant
Industrial Development Consultants Ltd v Cooley [1972] 1 WLR 443 Resignation to take a corporate opportunity is no escape
Bhullar v Bhullar [2003] EWCA Civ 424 An opportunity coming to a person as a director must be accounted for, even where the company had resolved not to pursue it
O’Donnell v Shanahan [2009] EWCA Civ 751 There is no “scope of business” limitation on a director’s fiduciary duty

8.1.4 Shareholders and minority protection

Case Proposition
Foss v Harbottle (1843) 2 Hare 461 The proper claimant for a wrong to the company is the company; the court will not interfere in matters the general meeting can ratify
Ebrahimi v Westbourne Galleries Ltd [1973] AC 360 Equitable considerations may make it unjust to insist on strict legal rights; the source of the quasi-partnership concept
Re Saul D Harrison & Sons plc [1995] 1 BCLC 14 Fairness is judged against the terms of the commercial relationship, ordinarily the articles
O’Neill v Phillips [1999] 1 WLR 1092 Unfairness requires a breach of the agreed terms or use of the rules contrary to good faith; there is no no-fault divorce; a fair buy-out offer defeats the petition
Re Bird Precision Bellows Ltd [1986] Ch 658 In a quasi-partnership exclusion case, shares are valued pro rata, without a minority discount
Prudential Assurance Co Ltd v Newman Industries Ltd (No 2) [1982] Ch 204 A shareholder cannot recover reflective loss
Johnson v Gore Wood & Co [2002] 2 AC 1 The rule confirmed and explained
Sevilleja v Marex Financial Ltd [2020] UKSC 31 The rule is a bright line confined to shareholders suing qua shareholder; it does not bar creditors’ claims
Borland’s Trustee v Steel Brothers & Co Ltd [1901] 1 Ch 279 A share is an interest measured by a sum of money, consisting of mutual covenants — not a share of the assets

8.1.5 Share capital, charges and distributions

Case Proposition
Trevor v Whitworth (1887) 12 App Cas 409 Capital may not be returned to members except as the law permits
Ooregum Gold Mining Co of India Ltd v Roper [1892] AC 125 Shares may not be issued at a discount to nominal value
Re Wragg Ltd [1897] 1 Ch 796 A private company may accept any consideration of value; the court will not inquire into adequacy
Greenhalgh v Arderne Cinemas Ltd [1946] 1 All ER 512 Subdivision affecting voting power is not a variation of class rights
White v Bristol Aeroplane Co Ltd [1953] Ch 65 A bonus issue diluting voting strength affects the enjoyment of rights, not the rights themselves
Re Yorkshire Woolcombers Association Ltd [1903] 2 Ch 284 Romer LJ’s three characteristics of a floating charge
Agnew v Commissioner of Inland Revenue (Re Brumark) [2001] UKPC 28 Construe the instrument, then categorise as a matter of law; the parties’ label does not decide it
Re Spectrum Plus Ltd [2005] UKHL 41 A book debts charge leaving the company free to draw on the proceeds is a floating charge; Siebe Gorman overruled
It’s a Wrap (UK) Ltd v Gula [2006] EWCA Civ 544 Section 847 requires knowledge of the facts, not of the law
Bairstow v Queens Moat Houses plc [2001] EWCA Civ 712 Directors are liable to restore the full amount of an unlawful distribution

8.1.6 Insolvency

Case Proposition
BNY Corporate Trustee Services Ltd v Eurosail-UK 2007-3BL plc [2013] UKSC 28 The balance sheet test is not mechanical; the question is whether the company has reached the “point of no return”
Re MC Bacon Ltd [1990] BCLC 324 A preference requires a desire to prefer, not mere knowledge of the effect; security given under commercial pressure to secure continued support is not a preference
Re Produce Marketing Consortium Ltd (No 2) (1989) 5 BCC 569 Wrongful trading: contribution measured by the increase in the net deficiency
Re Continental Assurance Co of London plc [2007] 2 BCLC 287 The liquidator must show the continued trading actually caused loss
Re Patrick and Lyon Ltd [1933] Ch 786 Fraudulent trading requires “actual dishonesty involving … real moral blame”
Re AGPS BondCo plc [2024] EWCA Civ 24 Cross-class cram down is discretionary; “no worse off” is a threshold, and the court scrutinises fair allocation between classes
