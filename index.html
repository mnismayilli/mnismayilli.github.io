About

I am a Senior Lecturer in Economics at the University of Oxford, where I lecture in Microeconomics and Public Economics in the Department of Economics and give microeconomics tutorials at The Queen's College. I supervise MPhil students in the department.

Before Oxford I taught at Warwick Economics, the University of Manchester, King's College London and London Business School, and completed my PhD at the University of Leicester.

My research is in industrial organisation and experimental economics: when firms strategically delay new products, how environmental regulation reshapes bundling in complementary-goods markets, and how risk attitudes and framing lead people to make decision errors.

  1. 01 Working paper · 2026

    Strategic Delay of Product Introductions

    A durable-goods monopolist can introduce a product later than a social planner would. With commitment the low-valuation consumers are over-delayed; without it the high types wait and the low types leave the market.

    With Subir Bose and Piercarlo Zanchettin
  2. 02 Working paper

    First-Order Stochastic Dominance Violations, Risk Preferences and Framing Effects

    An experiment with 140 participants: consistently risk-seeking people are markedly more likely to choose dominated lotteries, and a clearer frame removes the violations for everyone.

    Experimental economics
  3. 03 Work in progress

    Environmental Externalities and Strategic (Un)Bundling in Complementary Goods Markets

    When entrants from weakly regulated jurisdictions undercut the complement, an incumbent unbundles and shrinks its domestic footprint while global damage may rise.

    Environmental economics · Industrial organisation
